Contents

Executive summaryin one page.

Astra Thrust Labs is a business idea: to design, build and supply safe, reusable hybrid rocket engines and boosters, instrumented test stands and ready-to-teach curriculum to engineering colleges and research labs, either to buy or to rent. Instead of one-off, hand-built hardware, an institution gets a complete propulsion lab it can run, measure and extend.

We estimate that ₹1.5 crore is needed to build a test facility, a small rental fleet and three product classes, and to serve a first group of six institutions. In the base case the idea reaches operating break-even in Year 3 and about ₹5.7 crore of annual revenue in Year 5 (roughly ₹14.6 crore over five years), with recurring support, consumables and rentals making up a growing share. These are idea-stage estimates, not results.

01 · The problem

Propulsion is taught on slides, not on test stands.

Rocket propulsion sits at the heart of aerospace engineering, yet many students can graduate without ever seeing an engine fire under measurement. The equations are in the textbook; the hardware, the instrumentation and the safe place to use them are not on campus.

Colleges that want to change this meet the same wall: engines are expensive to import, slow to custom-build, and nobody on staff has the safety training to run them. Student teams burn a year building something that is never properly tested.

02 · Our solution

An engine, a test stand and a syllabus.Delivered to your campus.

We supply hybrid rocket engines, which keep fuel and oxidiser separate until firing and can be throttled or shut down on command. That makes them a more forgiving platform for teaching and research than most alternatives, and they are reusable across many test runs.

Every engine ships with a calibrated static test stand, data-acquisition software, a safety manual, lab experiments mapped to the aerospace syllabus and on-site training for faculty. Institutions with specific needs can order a custom configuration. Colleges that do not want to buy can rent engines, boosters and test stands by the month or for a single test campaign.

03 · Market & competition

Many students learn propulsion on paper.We want them to learn it on a stand.

Our first customers would be the aerospace, mechanical and mechatronics departments of engineering colleges, universities and research institutes that already run rocketry clubs or propulsion electives. Competitions and funded student projects could create steady demand for hardware, and for rentals around event dates.

  • Beachhead: departments with an active rocketry club or propulsion course.
  • Expansion: universities, polytechnics and state research labs.
  • Adjacent: private research groups needing small, test-ready engines.

The market is described by who we plan to serve, not by measured figures. Sizes would be validated through customer interviews during the pilot phase.

04 · Business model

Sell the hardware once.Earn from the lab every year.

Hardware sales

Spark, Vega and Orion units sold directly to institutions, the main source of Year 1–2 revenue.

Rent engines & boosters

Colleges rent engines, boosters and test stands by the month or for one test campaign, with no large upfront cost. Hardware is inspected and refurbished between rentals, so one unit can earn many times.

Annual support & consumables

Service plan, calibration, oxidiser refills and spare parts, a recurring stream that grows with every customer.

Custom engineering

Project-based work for research labs needing special configurations, priced per project.

Training & curriculum

Faculty workshops, lab manuals and certification for student teams.

Competition partnerships

Aim to be the engine and stand partner for student rocketry events, a low-cost way to reach many colleges, with rentals sized to the event.

  • Direct outreach to heads of aerospace and mechanical departments.
  • Demo roadshow with a portable Spark stand at partner colleges.
  • Official partnerships with student rocketry competitions.
  • Faculty workshops that convert into purchase orders.

Pricing logic

  • Priced to fit common department lab budgets and grants.
  • Rental (about 5% of the purchase price per month, minimum 3 months) removes the capital-expense barrier.
  • Service plan priced at about ₹1.2 L per year per institution.
  • Bundles of engine plus stand discounted against separate purchase.

    08 · Risks & safety

    Safety first.It is the product.

    Propulsion hardware is only valuable to a college if it is safe to run. We design around that: hybrid engines that can be shut off, remotely operated test bays, written procedures, mandatory training and supply only to accredited institutions under a usage agreement.

    Rules for handling propulsion hardware and its materials vary and change, so licensing and compliance with the relevant Indian authorities will be confirmed with legal counsel and advisers before any commercial sale.

    RiskLikelihoodImpactMitigation
    Safety incident at a customer siteLowHighHybrid design, remote ignition, mandatory training, usage agreements and insurance
    Regulatory delays or licence changesMediumHighEarly legal counsel, advisory board, begin with Spark stands and low-thrust classes
    Slow college purchasing cyclesHighMediumRental option, grant-linked sales, faculty champions, competition partnerships
    Component cost or supply swingsMediumMediumLocal sourcing, two suppliers per critical part, buffer inventory
    Rental fleet wear, damage or misuseMediumMediumInspection and refurbishment after each rental, refundable deposit, usage agreement and trained operators
    Technical delays in OrionMediumMediumStaged development on Vega learnings, faculty co-development
